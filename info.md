Start with a complete demonstration of your project's core functionality. Walk through the main features in a logical order — begin with login or onboarding, then move into the key workflows a user would actually follow.

Follow a natural flow: show your app the way a real user would experience it, not a random tour of screens.

2. Demonstrate Your Testing Process
Judges want to see your project actually working — not just a finished result. Show your testing happening live, in real time. Narration is optional — the important thing is that what you're testing is clearly visible on screen.

Enter data live: type inputs on screen during the recording instead of showing pre-filled forms or saved results.
Make your test cases visible: show what kind of input you're using, for example, a valid email versus an invalid one — through on-screen text, zoomed-in input fields, or narration, whichever works best for you.
Show success and edge cases: demonstrate how the app behaves with correct inputs as well as with errors or unusual inputs.
Be specific: make sure the exact values you're entering are clearly readable on screen, not just described vaguely.
Instead of quickly typing and moving on, pause briefly on the input so viewers can actually read it — e.g. clearly show "john@example.com" entered in the email field before you submit.

3. Highlight GenAI Integration
Since Generative AI is central to your solution, make its role unmistakable to anyone watching.

Point it out clearly: call out the exact moment and screen where AI is being used.
Show it working: display the prompt going in and the response coming out.
Explain its purpose: briefly describe what problem the AI is solving in your project.
Prove it's dynamic: show that responses change based on input, rather than being fixed or hardcoded.
4. Be Clear and Step-by-Step
Narration is not required — what matters is that each step is easy to follow, however you choose to present it (voiceover, on-screen text/captions, or simply a clear, well-paced screen recording).

Make actions visible: keep your cursor visible and pause briefly on important clicks or results so viewers can follow what's happening.
Structure it in steps: move through your project in a logical order — login, then dashboard, then the AI feature, and so on — so the flow is obvious even without commentary.
If you do narrate: speak clearly, at a moderate pace, and avoid long pauses or mumbling.
If you use on-screen text: keep it short, readable, and on screen long enough to read comfortably.
5. Keep It Concise
Your full video should be under 4 minutes. Plan your walkthrough so every section gets enough time without running long — a tight, well-paced video is more effective than one that tries to show everything.
